# Before you start

Through Veridian, we empower individuals and organizations to create a more secure web where data and interactions are verifiable.
We do this in a privacy-preserving way without any centralized platforms, preferring a decentralized, protocols-first approach.

Throughout this documentation, you will see several references to cryptographic identifiers.
They are similar to a wallet address on a blockchain in that they are controlled by cryptographic key pairs.
These key pairs may be used to digitally sign data so that the data can be attributed to a person or entity through its identifier.

<Callout type="info">
  We use the [Key Event Receipt Infrastructure](https://trustoverip.github.io/kswg-keri-specification/) (KERI) standard for managing identifiers and keys.
  Developers should refer to our [protocol guides](../protocols) for more information.
</Callout>

## Profiles

Later, you will see that users can create profiles within the Veridian Wallet.
Each profile is backed by an identifier, and profiles may be created for individuals or groups.
A user might choose to create multiple profiles which represent different personas of that person's digital identity.

For now, individual profiles use single-signature identifiers under the hood.
This means there is one key pair to sign attestations, and one key pair to rotate keys.
We hope to expand this in the future to support multi-device recovery and social recovery.

Group identifiers are of course already multi-signature.
Each member of the group controls one of the signing keys and one of the rotation keys.
A threshold can be set to decide how many members of a group are required to sign or rotate.

<Callout type="info">
  Multi-signature identifiers in theory can be customized much further.
  Each member's signature can be weighted, and the threshold and weights can be different for signing and rotation.
  In fact, you could even have different members who can sign and who can rotate.

  For now, we're keeping things simple to not overcomplicate the user experience!
</Callout>

## Rotating keys

Identifiers will build up reputation over time as they sign more data, and may also hold or issue important Verifiable Credentials.
Additionally, the more data you sign with a key, the more exposed it gets, either through its environment or cryptanalysis.

As such, it's important to be able to update the keys which relate to your identifiers over time.
This is known as key rotation and helps you recover your identity from key compromise, or prevent it from happening in the first place.
In the case of a group identifier, rotation also allows you to change which members are in the group.

The ultimate goal is to keep your identifiers for life.
That way you can always prove who you are and keep your reputation.
This is why we are so keen on being ready for any potential key compromise or device loss.

## Delegated identifiers

Single or multi-signature identifiers may also be delegated.
Delegation in KERI is known as co-operative delegation and can be used in enterprise for top-down hierarchical identifier recovery.
It also has other scalability benefits; for those who are interested in the various types of benefits, please check out [this section](../protocols/delegation) of our developer docs.

There are some advanced features of recovery that are enabled by delegated identifiers, but we think it would be a little too much for this documentation.
Delegated identifiers aren't supported in the wallet yet but are indeed planned for our next release!
